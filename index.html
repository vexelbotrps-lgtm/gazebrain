A QUICK FIELD GUIDE

Small gestures.
Useful actions.

Choose a movement that feels comfortable. EyeSync shows live eye ratios and hold progress in its local preview and command panel.

01NAVIGATION

Move the cursor

Move your head naturally to steer the pointer. Calibration helps map your movement to your screen.

Head movement
02PUSH-TO-TALK

Close both eyes

Hold both eyes closed for 1 second to tap Tab. Reopen for at least 0.25 seconds before the next tap.

Tab · one-second hold
03OPTIONAL CLICK

Smile to click

Hold a closed-mouth smile for 1.2 seconds to left-click. Relax your face to rearm.

Left click · optional
04OPTIONAL CLICK

Open your mouth

Hold your mouth open for 1 second to right-click. Relax your mouth to rearm.

Right click · optional

Facial click gestures are available through the setup workflow; direct tracker use keeps them off unless explicitly enabled.

YOUR FIRST SESSION

Start with what
you already have.

EyeSync runs on Windows with Python and a webcam. The guided setup checks calibration before opening the tracker and its local command panel.

01 Windows 10 / 11 02 Python 3.10–3.12 03 A webcam
Open the source repository ↗
01

Get the project

Download or clone the EyeSync repository, then open a terminal in its folder.

git clone https://github.com/vexelbotrps-lgtm/Face-Controlled-Operating-System.git
02

Install requirements

Install the Python packages listed by the project.

pip install -r requirements.txt
03

Calibrate, then launch

The setup checks required gaze calibration and opens EyeSync. Follow the on-screen instructions.

python setup_and_start.py

BUILT WITH PRIVACY IN MIND

Your camera stays
with your computer.

EyeSync processes camera frames locally and does not save webcam photos or video. Calibration and gesture samples are numeric local data. The site itself never asks for camera or microphone access.

If you use a third-party speech tool, its handling of audio is governed by that provider’s settings and privacy policy.
